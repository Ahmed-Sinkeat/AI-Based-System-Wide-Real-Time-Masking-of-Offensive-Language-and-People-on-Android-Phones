1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document explains what the app must do and how well it must do it. It is written for the project team and supervisors, so it avoids deep technical detail. The full technical study and the test results live in investigation-android-v1.md and p0-results-android-v1.md.

1.2 The problem

Some people want to avoid bad language and images of people while they use their phone: in comments, chats, social apps, videos, ads and news. Today's tools have three problems:

  • Most work only in one place, such as one browser or one website.
  • Tools that watch the whole screen show the content first and cover it after, so it flashes for a moment.
  • Some send screen content to a server, which is bad for privacy.

1.3 Our idea

The project is one Android app. It uses Android's accessibility feature (the same one screen readers use) to know what is on the screen, and it draws covers on top. Everything runs on the phone.

  • Bad language: the app reads the text that apps show, finds bad words with a word list, and covers them. From v1.1, our own small AI model on the phone adds context: it catches insults without bad words and avoids covering normal uses of words such as "dog".
  • People: the app looks at a picture of the screen and finds people with an AI model on the phone, then covers them. From v6 this also works for videos while they play.
  • Text inside images (v2): the app reads the text inside pictures (OCR), for example memes and thumbnails, and covers the bad words.

2. Overview

2.1 How it works, simply

Bad language (v1):

  • Listen: Android tells the app when the screen changes.
  • Read: the app reads the text on the screen.
  • Clean: it removes spelling tricks (for example tashkeel, stretched letters, symbols in place of letters).
  • Match: it looks each word up in the bad-word list.
  • Cover: it puts a solid block over the bad word or over the whole message (the user chooses).

Text inside images (v2): the app takes a picture of the other app's window, the OCR model reads the text in it, the word list (and from v1.1 our model) checks that text, and bad words are covered.

People (v3–v5): the changed part of the screen is covered at once. The app takes a picture of the other app's window (the picture does not include our covers), the AI model finds the people, and the cover is removed everywhere except over the people.

Content changesscroll, new page, new app
Covered while checkedpeople: about 0.3–0.7 s today
Shownwith every detected person hidden

Text needs no "covered while checked" step. The check takes less than a millisecond, so the cover appears right after the text.

2.2 Users

One kind of user: a person who chooses to install the filter on their own phone. It is a personal tool, not a parental-control or monitoring tool.

2.3 Where it runs

ItemRequirement
Systemv1 (text): Android 8.0 or newer. v2–v6 (everything that needs a picture of the screen): Android 14 or newer. Older phones are not supported for these versions.
Test phoneXiaomi Redmi 12: MediaTek Helio G88, 2 fast + 6 slow processor cores, small Mali-G52 graphics. A normal low-cost phone.
InternetNot needed. The app does not even ask for internet access.
InstallFrom an APK file during development. Google Play later: the team already has a Google Play developer account.

2.4 Assumptions

  • The user turns on the app in the phone's accessibility settings and allows it to run in the background.
  • "Bad language" means the words and phrases on our list, in four categories: swearing, sexual words, insults and blasphemy.
  • "Person" means any human figure: real, drawn, animated or in a game.
  • Hiding something by mistake is acceptable. Showing a person or a bad word by mistake is not. When in doubt, hide.

5. Not planned

  • iPhone. Apple does not let apps see or cover other apps.
  • Arabic dialects and Arabizi (Arabic written in Latin letters). Possible after v1.
  • A root mode that works inside Android's screen drawing
  • The Linux desktop version. It is paused as plan B, and its test results still help.
  • Hiding only some people, for example by gender. This is much less accurate, and a mistake means a person is shown.
  • Blocking the user from turning the filter off. It is a personal choice, not a lock.
  • Apps that block screenshots (banking, some video apps). They rarely show what we hide.
  • Phones older than Android 14 for v2–v6, for now.

8. Terms

FrameOne picture on the screen. The Redmi 12 shows up to 90 per second, so one frame is about 11 ms.
ms / sMillisecond (one thousandth of a second) / second
Accessibility serviceA special kind of Android app that the user turns on in settings. It can read what is on the screen and draw on top of it. Screen readers use the same feature.
CoverThe solid block or strong pixelation that we draw over a bad word or a person
Word listOur list of bad words and phrases, each tagged with a category
Cleaning (normalization)Removing spelling tricks before matching, so "كلبببب" and "كلب" count as the same word
OCRReading text from a picture. Used for text inside images in v2 (PaddleOCR).
AI modelA trained program. Ours find offensive text, read text in pictures, or find people.
TilesChecking the screen in a few parts instead of all at once, so small people look bigger to the model
Hide firstOur rule for people: new content stays covered until it is checked (in technical documents: "fail-closed")
