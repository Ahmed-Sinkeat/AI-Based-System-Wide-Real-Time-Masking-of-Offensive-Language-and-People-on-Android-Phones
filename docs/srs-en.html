1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document states what the app must do (functional requirements), how well it must do it (non-functional requirements), and how each requirement will be checked. It is the agreement between the team and the supervisor, and the reference for design, testing and the final report. The technical study and test results are in investigation-android-v1.md and p0-results-android-v1.md; the design is in app-design-v1.md.

1.2 Problem

Some people want to avoid bad language and images of people while they use their phone: in comments, chats, social apps, ads and news. Today's tools have four problems:

  • Most work in one place only (one browser, one website).
  • Tools that watch the whole screen show content first and cover it after. The eye understands a picture in as little as 13 ms[R6], so a late cover is too late.
  • Some send screen content to a server, which harms privacy.
  • Almost none filter Arabic text, which is full of dialect, spelling tricks and context-dependent words.

1.3 Product scope

The product is one Android app. It uses Android's accessibility service (the feature screen readers use) to read the screen and draw covers on top of other apps. Bad words are found with a word list plus our own small AI text model; people are found with an AI segmentation model. Everything runs on the phone; the app has no internet permission.

2. Overall description

2.1 Product perspective

A new, self-contained product. It does not replace another system. It sits between the apps on the screen and the user's eyes, using only public Android APIs (no root).

Other appsWhatsApp, Facebook, Chrome, YouTube… share their text with Android; their windows can be pictured
Our app (one accessibility service)text path: list + text model · image path: OCR + people model · one cover layer · settings
Usersets up, pauses, peeks; sees the screen with covers
Androidaccessibility events, text + per-letter boxes, window pictures, overlay
Phone hardwareCPU and GPU for the models; no NPU help on the test phone
No serverno internet permission; nothing leaves the phone

2.2 Product functions (summary)

  • Set up with clear consent, then turn on the accessibility service once.
  • Read the text on screen; clean it; match it against the word list; send unclear messages to the text model; cover the bad words.
  • Picture the app window; read text inside images (OCR) and find people; cover them.
  • Hide first where checking is slow (images, context words).
  • Covers follow scrolling; touches pass through them.
  • Control: on/off, pause, skipped apps, categories, languages, cover style, peek at one item.
  • Status: notification, Quick Settings tile, warning cards.
  • Later: self-control mode.

2.3 How it works, simply

Text (v1): Android reports a screen change → the app reads the visible text → normalizes it → looks up words in the list → clear bad words are covered at once; context words are covered at once and the text model decides whether the cover stays (FR-24) → covers drawn on each word's screen box.

Text in images (v2) and people (v3–v5): the changed area is covered at once → the app takes a window picture (which does not include our covers) → OCR reads text in images and the people model finds each person → covers stay only on bad words and people.

Content changesscroll, new page, new app
Covered while checkedimages: about 0.3–0.7 s today
Shownwith every detected person and bad word hidden

Why plain text is not covered first: the app learns what the text says from the same event that tells it the text appeared, and the list check takes under 1 ms. Covering all new text first would make the screen flash on every scroll without hiding anything sooner. The only gap is Android's event delay (NFR-3). Context words are the exception (FR-24) because the model takes longer.

2.7 User documentation

In-app only: setup screens (FR-1), a "try it" page with sample text, and an About & privacy page. The report and a demo video document the system for examiners.

2.8 Assumptions and dependencies

  • The user turns on the service in accessibility settings and allows background running (Xiaomi: Autostart, no battery limit).
  • "Bad language" means the entries of our reviewed list in four categories (swearing, sexual, insults, blasphemy), plus what our text model flags.
  • "Person" means any human figure: real, drawn, anime / cartoon, or in a game. Accuracy targets are first measured on photos; drawn and anime figures are measured as a separate group (see risk K-9).
  • Hiding too much is acceptable; showing what the user asked to hide is not. When unsure, hide.
  • Apps share their text with Android; where an app does not give per-word boxes, the whole message is covered.

Covers hide content without blocking the app. The user can deliberately uncover one item.
